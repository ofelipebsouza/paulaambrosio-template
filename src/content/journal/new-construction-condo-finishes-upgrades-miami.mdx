Miami's skyline keeps growing, and many of our clients buy their homes before the building is finished. Purchasing new construction is exciting: a brand new residence, modern amenities and often a spectacular view. But the months before closing are also the moment when the most important design decisions are made, and many buyers only think about the interior after they receive the keys.

Planning early saves time, money and a lot of demolition.

## Understand What the Developer Delivers

 Every building has its own standard of delivery. Some units arrive fully finished with flooring, kitchen and bathrooms. Others are delivered with a more basic package, and some leave floors and certain finishes to the owner. The first step is to read the specifications carefully and understand exactly what is included, which options exist and the deadlines for choosing them.

## Design Before You Receive the Keys

 The best moment to hire an interior designer is before closing, not after. With the floor plans in hand, we can develop the full layout, furniture plan, lighting and millwork. We use 3D renders so clients can see their home before it exists, and we order furniture with long lead times in advance. When the unit is delivered, installation can begin almost immediately.

## Know the Building Rules

 Condominium associations set rules for renovations, contractors, insurance, working hours and elevator use. New buildings often have additional restrictions in the first months after delivery, when many owners are moving in at the same time. We plan schedules around these rules from the start, as we explain in [Navigating Luxury High Rise Condominium Renovations](/journal/navigating-luxury-condominium-renovations-in-south-florida/).
