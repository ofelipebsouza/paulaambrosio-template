Yet many balconies and terraces remain an afterthought: two chairs, a small table and a view no one
enjoys. We design them as true extensions of the interior, with the same care we give a living room.

Start With How You Want to Live Outside
Before choosing a single piece of furniture, we ask simple questions. Is this a place for morning coffee,
for sunset cocktails, for family lunches or for reading alone? A narrow balcony in a Brickell tower and a
wide terrace in Bal Harbour ask for very different answers, but both deserve a clear purpose.

    •   Lounge zone for conversation, with a sofa or deep chairs and a low table.
    •   Dining zone for meals outdoors, placed close to the kitchen door.
    •   Quiet corner with a single lounge chair, a side table and the best view.

Even a small balcony can hold one of these zones beautifully when it is chosen with intention.

Furniture That Belongs Outdoors
Outdoor furniture in Miami must handle sun, salt, rain and wind. We choose teak, powder coated
aluminum and outdoor rope weaves, with cushions in solution dyed performance fabrics that dry quickly
and resist fading. Quick dry foam inside the cushions makes a real difference after a summer storm.

Weight matters too. On high floors, wind is stronger, so we favor pieces that are substantial and stable,
and we plan where lighter items will be stored when a storm warning comes.

Shade Is a Luxury
The Miami sun is generous, sometimes too generous. Depending on building rules, we use retractable
awnings, pergolas, large cantilever umbrellas or outdoor curtains to create comfortable shade. On
condominium balconies, association guidelines often define what is allowed, so we review them before
designing.

Light for the Evening
An outdoor room lives most beautifully at night. Low, warm lighting creates atmosphere without
attracting insects: lanterns, rechargeable table lamps, discreet uplights on plants and wall fixtures rated
for coastal exposure. We avoid bright overhead light that flattens the view and competes with the city
lights beyond.

Bring the Garden Up
Plants soften architecture and give privacy. Large planters with palms, olive trees, bougainvillea or
sculptural succulents bring life to a terrace, even many floors above the ground. We choose species that
tolerate wind and salt, and we plan irrigation and drainage so maintenance stays simple.

Connect Inside and Outside
The best outdoor spaces feel continuous with the interior. We repeat materials, colors and textures
from the living room outside, align flooring where possible and treat the sliding doors as a frame for the
view. When the doors open, the home doubles in size.
