Think of the best hotel room you have ever stayed in. The bed felt perfect, the room was silent and dark, everything you needed was exactly where your hand reached for it. That feeling is not luck. It is design, and it can be created at home.

We design primary suites with the same hospitality thinking we bring to hotels: every decision serves rest.

## Layout Comes First

 The bed should face the view or the most peaceful wall, never the door to the bathroom. We look for a clear path around the bed, space for a seating corner if the room allows, and a smooth sequence from

bedroom to dressing area to bath. When the layout is right, the room feels calm before a single piece of furniture arrives.

Light You Can Control Deep rest needs real darkness. We combine blackout shades with sheer curtains, so the room can move from full light to full dark with one touch. Lighting is layered: warm bedside lamps for reading, soft indirect light for the evening and a gentle path light for the night. Scenes programmed in advance, one for waking and one for sleeping, make the suite feel effortless.

Silence and Softness Hard floors and glass are beautiful but can make a bedroom echo. Large rugs, upholstered walls or headboards, heavy curtains and fabric covered furniture absorb sound and make the room feel protected. In high rise condominiums, we also consider sound from neighbors and elevators when planning finishes.

A Palette That Calms We favor quiet colors in the bedroom: warm whites, sand, clay, soft greens and muted blues inspired by the Miami coastline. Natural textures such as wood, linen, boucle and leather bring warmth without visual noise. Art should be restful too, chosen to be the last thing you see at night.
