Renovating a Miami Beach penthouse starts before you choose a countertop. Flooring, windows and even the route a slab takes up to your unit can change what is possible. The first thing I ask for is the building’s current renovation package.

## Get the building rules before drawing the plan

Ask management for the alteration agreement, submission checklist, work hours, insurance requirements and rules for protecting common areas. Confirm who makes the approval decision and when they review applications. If the scope touches windows, an exterior door or a shared system, identify what the declaration calls a common element before assuming the item can be changed. [Florida Statutes § 718.113](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0718/Sections/0718.113.html) addresses alterations to common elements.

## Check which city permit path fits the scope

Building consent and municipal permitting are separate. The City of Miami Beach provides a [checklist for nonstructural condo interior alterations](https://www.miamibeachfl.gov/condominium-interior-alteration/) and a [separate checklist for qualifying interior flooring projects](https://www.miamibeachfl.gov/commercial-condominium-online-flooring/). Ask the building department and your licensed contractor which checklist applies and whether your drawings or trade work need additional review. Confirm this before demolition and before committing to a material that the building may not accept.

## Put approvals, orders and installation on one calendar

I start with the measured plan and decisions that affect the drawings. Then I schedule building and city reviews, construction and required inspections, and the delivery and installation dates. An acoustic flooring specification or a bathroom waterproofing detail can affect the design before a finish order is placed. Elevator booking also matters well before installation day, especially for large stone pieces and furniture that cannot be broken down.

Ask your team to show what can move in parallel and what must wait for approval. Make one person responsible for updating the calendar when a decision changes. The right timeline is specific to your scope and your building.
