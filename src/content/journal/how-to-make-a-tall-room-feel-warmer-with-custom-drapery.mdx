A tall living room can feel dramatic and still be a place where you want to sit and stay. Drapery helps bring those two qualities together. At [HOME DR](https://www.paulaambrosio.com/projects/home-dr/), soft curtains frame the seating area and balance the dark stone fireplace without taking attention away from the architecture.

## Plan the track before you pick the fabric

I start with where the panels will hang and where they will stack when open. If there isn’t enough room for the fabric to gather at the sides, it can cover more glass than you intended. For very tall windows, I also coordinate how the track will be supported and how the panels can be taken down for cleaning. Those decisions are easier to make before the ceiling and trim are finished.

## Let the room tell you what to layer

A sheer can soften a bright room without losing the view, but it may offer little privacy after dark. In a Miami Beach or Sunny Isles Beach condo, I like to test the fabric against the glass during the brightest part of the day. In an Aventura bedroom, a separate blackout layer might matter more. And in a Coral Gables or Boca Raton living room with tall ceilings, I look at the fabric from across the room before deciding how full the panels should be.

My last check is always from the sofa. Does the light feel comfortable? Do the curtains frame the room without blocking the view? If I want to stay there a little longer, the drapery is doing its job.
