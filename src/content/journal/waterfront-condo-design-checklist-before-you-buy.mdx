The first thing you notice when you walk into a waterfront condo is usually the view. But will you see it from the sofa? Is there room for a dining table without blocking the terrace door? Those answers can change which residence makes the most sense for you.

At Paula Ambrosio Interior Design, our research can start before you buy. We look at how the home needs to work for your family or the way you live, then compare the shortlisted residences from a design perspective.

## Take this checklist to your next showing

1. **Start with your routines.** List the rooms you’ll use separately and together. Can guests stay without taking over a home office? Is there a quiet place for a call? Does the entry have somewhere for bags, shoes and beach gear?
2. **Test-fit the furniture.** Bring a dimensioned floor plan, a tape measure and the dimensions of pieces you want to keep. Mark where the sofa, dining table and beds would sit. Check that chairs pull out, doors open and the terrace stays accessible. If you’re buying before construction is complete, confirm which dimensions and finishes the seller can substantiate.
3. **Look at light from where you’ll sit.** Stand at the intended sofa, dining and desk positions. Note where the horizon appears, where direct sun hits and what neighbors can see. If practical, revisit at a different time to evaluate glare and privacy.
4. **Separate what can stay from what needs work.** Sort finishes, lighting, joinery and furnishings into keep, refresh or replace. Flag items that depend on others, like a floor replacement that might affect thresholds or built-ins. Have the appropriate professionals verify feasibility and likely costs before relying on them.
5. **Ask the building about logistics.** Request written information on alteration submissions, work hours, service-elevator dimensions, deliveries and contractor access. Ask which approvals or documents could affect the timeline. Use your agent, building management and qualified professionals to verify the answers.

## Compare the units side by side

Give each unit a column. Record the three things that work without changes, the three biggest changes you’d need, and the details you still need to verify. If a question is open, mark it as unknown instead of assuming the most favorable answer. The goal is to see which home gives you the daily life you want with the fewest compromises.
