Two towers on the same stretch of coast can have different renovation rules. In Bal Harbour and Sunny Isles Beach, I would keep a separate checklist for the association’s review, the municipality’s requirements and the actual delivery route. That prevents the two approvals from being confused. Before ordering flooring or scheduling demolition, ask management for the current package for your unit. The first question is what your building needs to review. The second is what your municipality requires for that scope.

## What I ask the building for

Ask for the alteration agreement, current contractor and insurance instructions, and the drawing and material requirements. Find out whether management requires flooring acoustics reports, waterproofing details or information about changes to plumbing and electrical work. Request the work hours, elevator booking process, and rules for staging materials, removing debris and protecting corridors. If there are deposits, ask how they are assessed and returned. These are useful questions, not a standard list of rules shared by every building.

If work could affect a shared riser, an exterior opening or another common element, check the declaration and take the question to management and the appropriate licensed professionals. [Florida Statutes § 718.113](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0718/Sections/0718.113.html) addresses approvals for certain alterations to common elements.

## Check the municipality separately

[Bal Harbour Village’s Building Department](https://balharbourfl.gov/doing-business/building-permits/) handles permit issuance and plan review for projects in the village. [Sunny Isles Beach’s Building Department](https://www.sibfl.gov/Building-Code/Building-Department/Building-Permits) asks condo owners to verify specific work and whether a licensed contractor is required. Ask the department that serves the property which documents, permits and inspections apply. An association’s permission does not replace a required permit.

For example, before selecting a new floor, ask whether the association needs a manufacturer’s acoustic report, a specific assembly or a written approval. Have the appropriate licensed professional verify the existing floor and the planned installation. Do not rely on a neighboring unit’s approval. Keep the applicable documents and written decisions together.

Before any order goes out, I ask the designer, contractor and building manager to review the same scope. It is much easier to catch a flooring or delivery restriction on paper than after the material has left the supplier.
