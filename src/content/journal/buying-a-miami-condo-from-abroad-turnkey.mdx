When you buy a Miami condo from abroad, the key design question is who can make the right decisions locally when you are in another time zone. A measured plan, a clear approval process and a realistic delivery sequence make remote work more manageable.

## Before closing: gather what the building can share

Make a short list of what you can confirm now and what needs a site visit. Request the floor plan, the list of finishes or furnishings included in the sale, and the building’s move and renovation rules. Ask your broker or manager when your design team can access the residence to verify dimensions. A sales plan helps us begin a conversation. Site measurements are what I want before committing to fitted millwork or a sofa that needs to clear a narrow elevator.

Before committing to furniture, ask your team to verify both the room dimensions and the delivery path: service elevator, hallway turns and your unit’s door. A piece can fit the room and still not make it upstairs.

Decide what you want ready on your first night. A functioning bedroom, privacy shades and enough lighting to unpack may matter more than having every accessory in place. This helps us prioritize orders and set a useful scope.

## Agree on how decisions will be made from abroad

We can review drawings and furniture layouts together on video. For the decisions that depend on feel, such as fabric, wood or stone, build in time to review physical samples. Agree on who will approve each purchase, how changes will be documented and which decisions can be delegated. One shared list of open decisions is far easier to manage than instructions scattered across messages.

## Set a first-night checklist before the first delivery

A turnkey scope can include product sourcing, ordering, delivery coordination, installation and final styling. If your condo needs construction, make sure your agreement clearly names who handles the work and the required building or municipal approvals. Ask who checks deliveries, what happens when a piece arrives damaged, and who will manage the last walkthrough. I would also agree on a handover list with care instructions, outstanding items and contact information before your arrival.

For one [Boca Raton high rise residence](https://www.paulaambrosio.com/projects/home-s/), the owner approved the design remotely before arriving for the reveal. We coordinated the layout, millwork, furnishings and styling as one scope.
