In Miami, the kitchen is never only a place to cook. It is where guests land with a glass of wine, where children do homework before dinner, where a Sunday lunch can last all afternoon. In the open floor plans that define so many South Florida homes and condominiums, the kitchen is also on display from the living room, the dining room and often the terrace.

That is why we design kitchens as living spaces first, with every working detail resolved so perfectly that it disappears.

## Light in Three Layers

 A kitchen needs light to work and light to feel beautiful. We combine recessed and architectural lighting for general illumination, linear lighting under cabinets for tasks, and decorative pendants over the island to give the room personality. Every layer is on its own dimmer, so the kitchen can move from a bright morning to a candlelit dinner. More on this in our article on [lighting design in Miami](/journal/lighting-design-miami-interior-design/).

## Planning a Kitchen in a High Rise

 Condominium kitchens bring their own rules. Associations define working hours and freight elevator reservations, and the elevator itself limits the size of stone slabs that can reach the unit. Venting options are often restricted, so hood selection must be resolved early. We manage all of this from the start, as we describe in [Navigating Luxury High Rise Condominium Renovations](/journal/navigating-luxury-condominium-renovations-in-south-florida/).

## The Details That Separate Good From Exceptional

 Outlets hidden in the island or under cabinets. A deep, generous sink with a faucet in a finish that ages well. Hardware that feels solid in the hand. Drawers instead of lower doors, so everything is visible and easy to reach. These are small decisions, but together they define how a kitchen feels every single day.
