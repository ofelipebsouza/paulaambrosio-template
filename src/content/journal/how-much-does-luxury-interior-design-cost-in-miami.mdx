The first question I ask when someone wants a number for Miami interior design is what they want done by the time they walk in. Designing a living room, furnishing an entire condo and renovating a residence with custom cabinetry are three different scopes. A single price per square foot can hide more than it explains.

## Build the budget in separate parts

**Design fees** cover the professional work described in your proposal. Ask which rooms are included and what you receive: layouts, material selections, renderings, drawings or coordination. Ask how many revisions are included and how changes affect fees.

**Furniture and finishes** may include seating, tables, lighting, window treatments, rugs, art and accessories. Decide what you are keeping and ask whether the shopping figures include taxes, freight and any procurement charges. An allowance is a planning figure until the items and prices are confirmed.

**Construction and custom work** should be easy to distinguish from the design fee. Ask your contractor for pricing based on the actual scope, especially for kitchens, bathrooms, electrical changes and built ins. If your building has special requirements, include them in the pricing conversation.

**Delivery and installation** can include receiving, inspection, storage, delivery, assembly and styling. Ask which are included and what would cost extra if orders arrive early or the building reschedules a delivery window.

## Compare two proposals line by line

Write down the rooms, the items you will retain and the must have work before reviewing proposals. Ask each team to mark what is included, what is an allowance and what is excluded. A lower design fee does not tell you whether the final project will cost less. Also agree how to set aside money for changes or surprises, particularly when the scope includes opening walls or altering existing work.
